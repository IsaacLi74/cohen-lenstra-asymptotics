\begin{abstract}
We tabulate the Sylow \(3\) subgroups of \(326{,}378{,}404\) imaginary
quadratic class groups with absolute fundamental discriminant at most \(2^{30}\).
The data contain 46 isomorphism types. We compare their frequencies with the
Cohen--Lenstra probabilities and fit their counting residuals with a free
exponent. Several common types have fitted exponents near \(5/6\).
The cyclic group \(C_{243}\) occurs \(1{,}104{,}084\) times, yet its fitted
exponent is \(0.5240\) over the full fitting window and \(0.6813\) over
its final two doublings. Comparing groups at similar occurrence counts
preserves this difference. We also check how the individual counts and fitted
coefficients combine into the divisibility count and the total size of
\(3\)-torsion.
\end{abstract}

\section{Counts and verification}

All sums are over negative fundamental discriminants \(-X\le D<0\).
Write \(G_D=\Cl(\mathbb Q(\sqrt D))[3^\infty]\), \(h_D=|\Cl(\mathbb Q(\sqrt D))|\)
and \(r(G)=\dim_{\mathbb F_3}(G/3G)\). Let \(N_G(X)\) count the fields with
\(G_D\cong G\), let \(N(X)\) count all fields, and let \(R_r(X)\) count
the fields of \(3\)-rank \(r\). If the full class group has cyclic factors
of orders \(d_i\), the Sylow factors have orders \(3^{v_3(d_i)}\), omitting factors
of order one. The raw input consists of 16 LMFDB shards \cite{lmfdb}. Their SHA-256 hashes
and the 227 cumulative bounds are recorded in the
\href{https://github.com/IsaacLi74/cohen-lenstra-asymptotics/blob/c8b1d72/data/processed/p3_statistics.manifest.json}{data manifest}.

We checked these identities at every checkpoint.
\begin{align}
N(X)&=\sum_G N_G(X),\\
\Ndiv(X)&=\sum_{G\ne 1}N_G(X)=N(X)-N_1(X),\\
R_r(X)&=\sum_{r(G)=r}N_G(X),\\
S_3(X)&=\sum_{-X\le D<0}|G_D[3]|
     =\sum_G 3^{r(G)}N_G(X)=\sum_r3^r R_r(X).
\end{align}
For an independent field-count check, let \(Q(Y;a,m)\) count squarefree
positive integers at most \(Y\) in the odd residue class \(a\) modulo
\(m\), with \(m\in\{2,4,8\}\). The M\"obius function \(\mu\) gives
\[
Q(Y;a,m)=
\sum_{\substack{d\le\sqrt Y\\d\;\mathrm{odd}}}
\mu(d)\left\lfloor\frac{\lfloor Y/d^2\rfloor+m-a}{m}\right\rfloor .
\]
This follows by expanding the squarefree indicator and using
\(d^2\equiv 1\pmod 8\) for odd \(d\). The fundamental discriminant count is
\[
N(X)=Q(X;3,8)+Q(X;7,8)+Q(X/4;1,4)+Q(X/8;1,2).
\]
It agreed with the dataset at all 227 checkpoints.

At \(X=2^{30}\), the divisibility count is \(139{,}775{,}918\), or
\(42.8263\%\) of all fields, and the torsion sum is \(638{,}841{,}092\).

\section{Residuals and fitting}

Set \(\rho=3/\pi^2\), \(\eta_3=\prod_{j\ge1}(1-3^{-j})\), and
\(P_{\CL}(G)=\eta_3/|\Aut(G)|\) \cite{wood}. For
\(G=\prod_i C_{3^{a_i}}\), let \(s_j=\#\{i:a_i\ge j\}\) and
\(m_j=\#\{i:a_i=j\}\). We compute
\[
|\Aut(G)|=3^{\sum_j s_j^2}\prod_{j\ge1}\prod_{k=1}^{m_j}(1-3^{-k}).
\]
The three counting residuals are
\begin{align*}
E_G(X)&=N_G(X)-\rho P_{\CL}(G)X,\
E_{3\mid h}(X)&=\Ndiv(X)-\rho(1-\eta_3)X,\
E_{\mathrm{tor}}(X)&=S_3(X)-2\rho X.
\end{align*}

We fit \(E(X_i)\approx cX_i^\theta\) by minimizing the unweighted sum of
squared errors in count units. With \(t_i=X_i/X_{\max}\), profiling out the
coefficient gives
\[
a(\theta)=\frac{\sum_i E(X_i)t_i^\theta}{\sum_i t_i^{2\theta}},
\qquad
c(\theta)=a(\theta)X_{\max}^{-\theta}.
\]
The primary fit lets \(\theta\) vary. The fixed \(5/6\) fit is a separate
diagnostic. A boundary optimum is reported as unavailable. We flag types
with fewer than 1,000 endpoint observations as sparse. The checkpoints
are cumulative and correlated, so the RMSE measures descriptive fit quality.

The full window uses the 116 recorded checkpoints from \(1{,}030{,}400\)
through \(2^{30}\). The late window uses the 26 recorded checkpoints in
\([2^{28},2^{30}]\). Plots retain both the observed proportion
\(N_G(X)/N(X)\) and the log magnitude of \(E_G(X)\). Fits use the signed
residuals before taking logarithms for display.

\paragraph{Comparing groups at similar counts.}
At about 1.1 million observations per group, the late window
\([X_{\max}/4,X_{\max}]\) gives free exponents
\(0.802,0.820,0.862,0.811\) for \(C_3,C_9,C_{27},C_{81}\), respectively.
For \(C_{243}\), it gives \(0.681\). With the exponent fixed at \(5/6\),
the residual norm divided by \(\|E_G\|_2\) ranges from \(1.24\%\) to
\(1.78\%\) for the four comparison groups and is \(5.72\%\) for
\(C_{243}\). On the final doubling alone, the free estimate for \(C_{243}\)
rises to \(0.728\). At the exact bound \(X=10^6\), it has 393 occurrences
against a CL main term of about 1,051. The \(C_3\) count at that bound
is 80,115 against about 85,129. These comparisons show that a single
\(X^{5/6}\) term fits \(C_{243}\) poorly over the measured windows,
even at a comparable occurrence count. The drift across windows leaves
its limiting exponent open.

\paragraph{Rank three.}
The rank-three total has free estimates \(0.884\) and \(0.881\) in the
full and late windows. There is also evidence that a lower-order term can
matter on this range. Training on \(10^6\le X\le2^{29}\) and evaluating on
the 13 later checkpoints, the RMSE is about 584 fields for \(bX^{5/6}\),
103 for \(cX^\theta\), and 55 for \(bX^{5/6}+dX^{2/3}\). The two-power
comparison illustrates how an effective exponent near \(0.88\) can arise
over a finite range.

\paragraph{Adding the group coefficients.}
Fixed-exponent least squares is linear in the data when the checkpoints
and exponent are shared. For \(\theta=5/6\), the fitted coefficients
therefore satisfy
\[
\widehat b_{3\mid h}=\sum_{G\ne1}\widehat b_G,
\qquad
\widehat b_{\mathrm{tor}}=\sum_G 3^{r(G)}\widehat b_G,
\]
when the CL mass of the unobserved types is included. Those types have zero
counts in the data and contribute the negatives of their main terms.
The observed nontrivial types sum to \(-0.11306846\), with an omitted
contribution of \(-0.00010027\), giving \(-0.11316873\).
The observed types contribute \(-0.41423970\) to the torsion coefficient
and the omitted contribution is \(-0.00054183\). The resulting
\(-0.41478154\) agrees with the direct fit and is close to the proved
torsion coefficient \(\kappa\approx-0.414786\) \cite{tt}.
